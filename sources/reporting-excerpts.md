# Secondary reporting excerpts

## Tom's Hardware

Source: https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/russian-disinformation-bot-traffic-nearly-eliminated-overnight-after-ukraine-drone-strikes-cripple-multiple-of-russias-google-data-centers-ukraine-intensifies-bombing-of-yandex-data-centers-in-response-to-moscows-strikes-on-kyiv-data-centers
Captured: 2026-10-12 UTC

The article reports that Cloudflare showed automated traffic from the Yandex network at 0.4% of the maximum number of HTTP requests in the prior two weeks. It says the series initially fell to about 57% on 8 October, and describes later attacks affecting Kaluga and a third facility.

Caveat: the article's headline and framing call this “disinformation bot traffic,” but the chart described is a network/request classification. The article image is illustrative and should not be treated as evidence of a data-center photograph. The underlying Radar series was not exported in this initial archive.

## Cybernews

Source: https://cybernews.com/news/yandex-data-centers-bot-traffic-drop/
Captured: 2026-10-12 UTC

Cybernews says the Cloudflare figure concerns automated requests from Yandex AS13238, not social-media messages. It notes that Russia-wide bot volume was largely unchanged and that covert influence operations commonly use residential proxies, compromised devices, VPNs, or foreign cloud providers. It also reports that Yandex's traffic began recovering.

Interpretation: this is the clearest published caution against treating the metric as a troll-farm census, though its time series should be independently archived.

## BBC

Source: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cw0k0g57mk3zo
Captured: 2026-10-12 UTC

BBC reports that Yandex paused operations at a Vladimir data center after damage in a Ukrainian drone strike, following earlier incidents at Sasovo and Kaluga. It reports service interruptions and Yandex's statement that it was working to restore connectivity.
